# Photos, videos and the frame-by-frame player

Status: implemented except side-by-side compare and long clips (#72), see [milestone 2](../plans/2026-10-23-milestone-2-media-and-evaluations.md)
Date: 2026-10-23
Updated: 2026-10-28
Decision: [0003](../adrs/0003-phone-converts-videos-before-upload.md). Evidence: [video-prototype.md](video-prototype.md).

- Stored by Django on the server's disk (default), served only through views that check the same permissions (no public media URLs); can be switched to S3-compatible object storage by configuration when the disk fills up (possible later, not built). Size limits and the per-user quota (`DJANGO_MEDIA_QUOTA_MB`) are in `server/config/settings.py`
- **The phone does the photo and video processing, not the server** (decided after the prototypes #35 and #70, [video-prototype.md](video-prototype.md)). A clip of 200 MB is not uploaded, converted and downloaded again. Right after a video is picked from the gallery, a native plugin (Media3 Transformer) converts it on the phone into a **frame-steppable version**: 720p H.264, no audio, **all frames kept at the capture frame rate on the real timeline** (a 120 fps slow-motion recording stays 120 fps and 1x is real time), a keyframe every 10 frames, no metadata that matters (no GPS position), plus a thumbnail. Only that file is uploaded (a few MB; limit 60 seconds and 50 MB per clip by default). The original stays in the phone's gallery. Photos are scaled and compressed on the phone before upload. The server stores and serves the files and checks size, type and permissions; it does no conversion.
- Uploads are small after the conversion, so they finish in seconds and use the same queue with retry as the sessions (and wait for Wi-Fi by default, changeable in settings). A plain upload from the page stalls when the app goes to the background (tested with 209 MB), so a chunked, resumable upload by a native plugin is only needed if clips get long.

## Moving into a session, and media without one

A photo or video belongs to an archer and may be in one session. Added from the archer's page it has none; it can be moved later and taken out again (#91).

- **Who**: `can_change` decides (the archer, whoever added it, a coach of a profile without an account). A coach cannot move an archer's own clips.
- **Server**: a repeated `PUT /api/media/<id>/` with `session` (empty: none) moves it; `validate_session` refuses another archer's session. The **notes on the clip move with it** (`Note.session` is set, `end` cleared) in the same transaction: a note takes the session of its clip, so the old notes would otherwise stay on the old session's page. `GET /api/media/` has `without_session` and `taken_after` / `taken_before`.
- **App**: "Move to session" in the photo view and the player, "Select" in the grid for several at once (one `PUT` each, failures are counted). `SessionPicker` lists the sessions of the archer from the local log and the server (30 days either side, "Show older"), the one around `taken_at` first. A clip the server has can not go into a session that is still waiting to be uploaded (it is greyed); a clip on the phone can, it waits like it did before.
- **Not in a session**: a chip on the archer's media page. A deleted session leaves its media behind (`SET_NULL`), they show up there; the delete confirmation says so.
- **Hint on the session screens**: `UnattachedHint` offers the media of the archer that was taken from one hour before the start to one hour after the end (an unfinished session counts as 4 hours, like the merge) with a one-tap "Add". On the ends screen, which must not scroll, it is one line linking to the archer's page with the chip on.

## Profile picture

Not a `media` row. `PUT`/`DELETE /api/me/avatar/` (and `/api/teams/<t>/archers/<id>/avatar/` for a profile without an account) take one JPEG of up to 512 KB; the server opens it with Pillow, checks that it is a JPEG of at most 1024 px, and saves it again (no EXIF, at most 512 px). The phone sends the middle square at 256 px. The file is served at `/api/users/<id>/avatar/` behind a signed link for one viewer and one person (an hour, the rights are checked again on every request). It is in the data export and the media zip (`avatar.jpg`), and "Delete my data" and the deletion of the account remove the file. Plan: [2026-10-28-profile-picture.md](../plans/2026-10-28-profile-picture.md).

## Video analysis player (frame by frame, like VisualEyes)

- **Controls**: play/pause; speed 0.1x to 2x; **step one frame back/forward** with buttons (hold to repeat) and arrow keys on web; a **jog strip** you swipe for fine scrubbing; frame counter and time shown (e.g. `frame 143 / 1800`).
- **Loop A to B** around one shot, e.g. from set-up to follow-through.
- **Coach notes on a photo**: a coach can write a note and draw on a photo, in the same format and with the same checks as on a frame (`frame` and `time_ms` stay empty). A photo opens in the player route `/media/:id` (the same view as a clip, with the picture, pencil and notes sheet, and previous/next among the photos). The swipe view in the photo grid shows the saved drawings over the photo and has a Notes button that opens the player. The notes list (session and archer pages) shows the photo with its drawing. Deleting a photo or a clip deletes its notes and drawings with it; the delete button says so when there are notes.
- **Coach notes on a frame**: a note stores the frame number and time, and tapping it jumps to that exact frame. A coach can also **draw on the frame** (lines, angles with their degrees, circles, five colours; e.g. for bow-arm or draw-elbow alignment), saved with the note. Points are fractions of the picture, checked by `notes/drawing.py`. Single shapes cannot be moved yet, only undone from the last one.
- **Not built**: two clips side by side with synced stepping (e.g. before/after a form change), and a ghost overlay.
- **How it works**: the phone reads the clip's capture frame rate (phones write it in the file for slow motion, `com.android.capture.fps`) and converts to a frame time equal to it, on the real timeline, so 1x is real time and a step is one frame (8.33 ms at 120 fps); the server stores the capture rate. Because the frame times are not exactly `i / fps`, a step is relative to the frame shown: read its `mediaTime` (from `requestVideoFrameCallback`) and seek to `mediaTime + 1.25 * frame_duration`, i.e. 25% into the next frame (aiming at the middle shows the wrong frame at some keyframe boundaries). Scrubbing and steps show single frames on a canvas decoded with WebCodecs (own MP4 reader `src/lib/mp4-samples.ts`, frames decoded ahead in `src/lib/frame-decoder.ts`); without WebCodecs the `<video>` seeks, one seek at a time. The player runs in the WebView on phones. Verified on a real 120 fps clip in the Android WebView (Chrome 155): frame-exact ([video-prototype.md](video-prototype.md)).
- **Limits**: only the Android WebView has been tested. In other browsers a step can occasionally show the neighbouring frame (not verified). Clips are converted on the phone, so frame numbers do not depend on the original's variable frame rate. Open: the iOS WKWebView, a very long clip, fast flicks (#72).
- **Prototype result (2026-10-23, [video-prototype.md](video-prototype.md))**: the player stays in the WebView (frame-exact on a 120 fps clip, 35 to 80 ms per step once keyframes are every 10 frames). The app has no "Take video" button in the picker (`capture` does not open the camera); the Add sheet offers recording through the camera plugin and choosing from the gallery. The conversion strips the GPS position.
- **Size**: the frequent keyframes make files larger than normal. The first estimate was 40 to 80 MB per 60-second 720p clip; the prototype's slow-motion test clip came out at 1.5 to 5 MB for 7 seconds but hardly moved, so the estimate is probably too high and is not verified with real archery clips. The bitrate follows `src/lib/video-plan.ts`.
